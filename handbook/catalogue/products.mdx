### Reading the status chip

The chip in each row comes from the product's status **and** its stock:

| Chip | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Hidden from customers, whatever the stock says. |
| **Out of stock** | Stock is zero. Visible, but can't be bought. |
| **Low stock** | Five or fewer left. Time to reorder. |
| **Active** | On sale and in stock. |

<Tip>
  Filter by **Low stock** once a week. Reordering at five is a calm decision;
  reordering at zero is a lost sale.
</Tip>

## Adding a product

Press **Add New Product**. The form is one panel — here's what each field does.

### The basics

| Field | What to put in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Product name** | What customers see. *"Kundan Rani Haar"*, not *"NK-001"*. |
| **Short tagline** | One line under the name on the storefront. |
| **SKU** | Your private code, e.g. `JR-NK-001`. Customers never see it. Never reuse one. |
| **Category** | Which collection it belongs to. Set these up in [Categories](/catalogue/categories) first. |
| **Price (₹)** | The normal price. |
| **Sale price (₹)** | Optional. Fill it in and the storefront shows the original struck through. Leave blank when not on offer. |
| **Stock** | How many you physically have. |
| **Status** | **Active** to sell it, **Draft** to keep it hidden. |
| **Badge** | Optional ribbon: **Bestseller**, **New**, **Bridal Edit**, or **None**. |

<Warning>
  **Stock is a real number, not a guess.** It's what stops you selling a piece
  you don't have. Sales reduce it automatically; cancellations put it back. Your
  job is to raise it when new stock physically arrives.
</Warning>

### The description fields

These all appear on the product page and are what actually sells the piece:

- **Description** — the main paragraph.
- **Material** — e.g. *"22K gold-tone, anti-tarnish"*.
- **Stones** — e.g. *"Kundan, pearls, cubic zirconia"*.
- **Care instructions** — e.g. *"Keep dry; store in a soft pouch"*.
- **Shipping information** — dispatch and returns detail for this piece.

<Tip>
  Care and materials matter more in artificial jewellery than anywhere else.
  A buyer who knows to keep it dry is a buyer who doesn't come back
  disappointed in three weeks.
</Tip>

### Plating finish

Offer the finishes this piece comes in. Three are one click — **Gold tone**,
**Silver tone**, **Rose gold** — and you can add your own labels (*"Antique
gold"*) up to eight in total. The customer picks one at checkout.

### Designs & images

Up to **six** photos per product.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload image">
    Add a photo and give it a design name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set primary">
    One photo is the **Primary** — the thumbnail used in listings and search.
    Pick your best-lit, clearest shot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Replace or Remove">
    Swap a photo without losing the entry, or delete it entirely.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Shoot on a plain, light background in daylight. Six consistent photos look
  like a shop; six mismatched ones look like a marketplace listing.
</Tip>

Press **Save Product** when you're done.

## Editing

Click any row, change what you need, save. Changes are live on the storefront
immediately — including price changes, so double-check before saving.

## Taking a product down

Set its status to **Draft**. It vanishes from the storefront and keeps
everything — photos, description, its sales history in
[Analytics](/insight/analytics).

Draft is almost always the right move. Reach for delete only for a listing
created by mistake.

## Adding many products at once

Setting up your catalogue for the first time, or doing a price revision across
fifty pieces? Don't do it one at a time —
[edit them in a spreadsheet](/catalogue/bulk-editing).

## Common situations

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A customer says a product page is out of date">
    Edit and save; the storefront updates immediately. If they're still seeing
    the old version, ask them to refresh.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I sold a piece in person, not through the site">
    Reduce its stock by hand in the product editor. The console only knows about
    orders placed on the website.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Two products ended up with the same SKU">
    Change one. Duplicate SKUs make your own catalogue searches useless and turn
    spreadsheet imports into guesswork.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Should I use sale price or a coupon?">
    **Sale price** for a permanent markdown on one piece. **[Coupon](/catalogue/coupons)** for a
    time-limited campaign across the shop. Coupons are far easier to end
    cleanly.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I feature a piece on the homepage?">
    Give it a badge, and check the homepage settings in
    [Settings](/setup/settings) — the hero and promo blocks there are what
    customers see first.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
