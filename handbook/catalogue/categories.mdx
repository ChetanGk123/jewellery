Categories are how a customer who doesn't know what they want finds something
anyway — *Bridal Sets*, *Necklaces*, *Earrings*. Every product belongs to one.

## Adding one

Press **New Category** and fill in:

| Field | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | What customers see in the menu, e.g. *"Bridal Sets"*. |
| **Description** | A line shown at the top of that collection's page. |
| **Photo** | The image on the category card. Upload one, or **Replace photo** later. |

The web address is built from the name automatically — *"Bridal Sets"* becomes
`yourshop.com/bridal-sets`. You never type it.

## Editing

Click a category, change it, **Save Changes**. Products stay attached.

<Warning>
  **Renaming changes the web address.** Anyone who bookmarked or shared a link
  to the old name will land on a missing page. Fine early on; think twice once a
  category has been live and shared for months.
</Warning>

## Deleting

The **Delete** button asks you to confirm. Only remove a category you're sure
about — the products in it need somewhere else to go.

<Note>
  Reassign the products first, then delete. A product without a category is
  hard for customers to stumble across.
</Note>

## How many to have

Fewer than you think. Six to ten clear collections beat twenty overlapping ones
— a customer facing twenty choices picks none.

<Tip>
  Name categories the way your customers speak, not the way your stockroom is
  organised. *"Bridal Sets"* is a category. *"Supplier B — 2024 lot"* is not.
</Tip>
