Customers can review pieces they've bought. Nothing they write appears on your
storefront until **you** approve it.

## The tabs

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Pending" icon="clock">
    Waiting for your decision. Nobody but you can see these. This is the menu
    badge.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Approved" icon="circle-check">
    Live on the product page for every visitor to read.
  </Card>
  <Card title="All" icon="list">
    Everything, including ones you rejected.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Approving or rejecting

Each pending review shows the star rating, what the customer wrote, which
product it's about and who wrote it. Two buttons:

- **✓ Approve** — publishes it on that product's page immediately.
- **× Reject** — keeps it hidden. The customer isn't told.

There's also **Contact reviewer**, which pulls up that customer's phone and
email — useful before you decide.

## What to approve

<Tip>
  **Approve honest three- and four-star reviews.** A page of nothing but
  five stars reads as fake and sells less than a page with a couple of measured
  criticisms on it.
</Tip>

Reject only what's genuinely unusable:

- Abuse, or anything that names and attacks a person
- Someone else's phone number, address or private details
- Content that has nothing to do with the piece
- Obvious spam or a competitor's link

<Warning>
  **Don't reject a review just because it's negative.** If a customer says the
  clasp broke in a week, that's information — for you and for the next buyer.
  Fix the piece, and use **Contact reviewer** to make it right with them.
</Warning>

## A bad review that's your fault

The fastest recovery isn't rejecting it. It's:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Approve it">
    Visible criticism you've clearly responded to builds more trust than a
    suspiciously clean page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use Contact reviewer and phone them">
    Replacement, refund, or an honest apology.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix whatever caused it">
    If it's a materials problem, it will keep coming back until you deal with
    the supplier.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Common situations

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I edit what a customer wrote?">
    No, and you shouldn't be able to. Your only choices are publish or don't.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I approved something by mistake">
    Open the **Approved** tab and reject it. It disappears from the storefront.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A review complains about delivery, not the product">
    Judgement call. It's fair information for buyers, but if the courier was at
    fault it may say more about them than your pieces. Either way, fix the
    courier problem.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Nobody is leaving reviews">
    Your delivery email already invites them. Beyond that, a personal WhatsApp a
    week after delivery — asking how the piece was, mentioning a review would
    help — works far better than any automatic reminder.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
