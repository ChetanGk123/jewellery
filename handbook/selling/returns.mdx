When a customer asks to return something, it appears here as a card. Each card
carries the order number, who the customer is, what they said, and any photos
they attached.

## The path a return takes

```
Requested → Approved → Received → Refunded (or Exchanged)
                    ↘
                      Rejected
```

Each card only shows the buttons that are legal at its current stage, so you
can't accidentally refund something you never got back.

## Step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the request">
    The card shows the customer's reason in their own words, whether they want a
    **refund** or an **exchange**, the order total, and any photos they
    uploaded. Click a photo to see it full size.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Approve or reject">
    **✓ Approve return** tells the customer to send the item back, and emails
    them the instructions.

    **× Reject** declines it. There's a note box above these buttons — anything
    you type there is **shown to the customer** when you reject, so give them a
    real reason.

    <Warning>That note is the one piece of text on this screen the customer reads. "Outside the return window — delivered 21 days ago" is fair. Blank is not.</Warning>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Wait for the parcel, then mark it received">
    When the item physically arrives back and you've checked its condition,
    press **Mark item received**.

    Inspect before you press. Once it's marked received, the next step is paying
    out.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Settle it">
    Two ways to finish:

    **Record UPI refund…** — enter the **amount in ₹** and the **UTR reference**
    from your UPI app, then press **Record refund**. Both are required; the
    console won't let you record a refund you can't evidence.

    **Mark exchanged** — for when you're sending a replacement piece instead of
    money back.

    Either way the customer is emailed automatically.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Refund before or after the item arrives?

The console assumes **item first, money second** — that's why refunding is only
possible after **Received**.

If you'd rather refund a trusted regular immediately, pay them by UPI now, and
record it here with the UTR once the item lands. The record stays accurate
either way.

## What gets kept

Every settled return keeps its evidence on the card:

- Refunds show the **amount, the UTR reference and the date**.
- Exchanges and rejections show the date they were settled.
- Any note you wrote stays attached.

This is your record if a payment is disputed months later. Don't rely on your
UPI app's history alone.

## Setting your return policy

The rules customers see — **how many days** they have after delivery, and **who
pays return shipping** — are in
[Settings → Returns](/setup/settings#returns).

<Note>
  Changing the return window affects future requests, not ones already open.
</Note>

## Common situations

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The returned item arrived damaged">
    Don't mark it received yet. Photograph it, phone the customer, and agree
    what happens. If you're declining after inspection, that's a conversation —
    the console has no "reject after received" step, so settle it by phone and
    record the outcome as a note on the original order.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The customer wants an exchange but you're out of stock">
    Phone them and offer a refund instead. Then record it as a refund here.
    Their original preference doesn't lock you in.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The request is outside my return window">
    Reject it, and write the reason in the note box — the customer sees it. Or
    approve it anyway as a goodwill gesture; the choice is always yours.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I refunded a different amount than the order total">
    That's fine — you type the amount. Partial refunds (keeping a restocking
    charge, or refunding one item from a set) are recorded exactly as you enter
    them.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The photos won't load">
    Reload the page. The photos are stored privately and their viewing links are
    short-lived, so a page left open for hours will show a "photos unavailable"
    message until refreshed.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
