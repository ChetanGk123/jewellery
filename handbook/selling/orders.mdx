This is the screen you'll live in. Everything else in the console supports what
happens here.

## The five stages

Every order walks the same path, one step at a time:

```
Pending → Confirmed → Packed → Shipped → Delivered
```

| Stage | What it means | What you do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pending** | Just arrived. You haven't accepted it. | Check it, then Confirm. |
| **Confirmed** | You've accepted it. The customer has been emailed. | Pick and pack the pieces. |
| **Packed** | Boxed and ready for the courier. | Hand it over, get the AWB. |
| **Shipped** | With the courier, tracking number recorded. | Wait for delivery. |
| **Delivered** | Done. | Nothing — this is final. |

**Cancelled** sits outside that line. An order can be cancelled at any stage
before it's delivered.

<Warning>
  **Pending is not "accepted".** A Pending order is a customer sitting there
  wondering whether you got it. They receive no confirmation email until you
  move it to Confirmed — so clear Pending first, every morning.
</Warning>

## Finding an order

The list shows orders from the **last two days** by default, which is what you
usually want.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Status tabs" icon="filter">
    Along the top: **All**, and one tab per stage. Click **Pending** to see only
    what needs accepting.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Search" icon="magnifying-glass">
    Type an order number, customer name, phone number or email. Results narrow
    as you type.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Date range" icon="calendar">
    Set your own **from** and **to** dates, or click **All dates** to drop the
    two-day window entirely.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Export CSV" icon="file-arrow-down">
    Downloads every order — not just the page you're looking at — as a
    spreadsheet, for your accountant or your own records.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  A customer on the phone asking "where's my order?" — search their **phone
  number**. It's the fastest way in, and they'll always know it.
</Note>

## Working a single order

Click any row to slide open the order panel. Everything you can do to an order
lives here.

### Advance it one stage

The big button carries the next step's name — **Mark as Confirmed**, then
**Mark as Packed**, and so on. The progress track along the top shows where the
order is.

Each move is real: the customer gets an email at Confirmed, at Shipped and at
Delivered.

### Step back if you mis-clicked

There's a one-step-back control for when your finger slipped. It takes the order
back exactly one stage.

<Warning>
  Stepping back from **Shipped** clears the courier details you saved. You'll
  need to enter the AWB again to ship it. Delivered and Cancelled can't be
  stepped back at all — they're final.
</Warning>

### Record the courier tracking number

Once an order is Packed, a courier box appears asking for the **AWB number**,
with an optional **tracking link**.

<Info>
  **The AWB is required before Shipped.** This is deliberate: the customer's
  shipping email contains that number, and an email telling them their parcel is
  on its way with nothing to track is worse than no email.
</Info>

### Print the paperwork

From **Confirmed** onwards, two print buttons appear:

- **Print invoice** — the customer's bill, goes in the parcel.
- **Print packing slip** — your own pick list for packing the right pieces.

Both open a clean printable page. Use your browser's print dialog, or save as
PDF.

<Tip>
  Nothing to print on a Pending order — you haven't accepted it yet — and
  nothing on a Cancelled one. That's why the buttons only appear from Confirmed.
</Tip>

### Leave a note for yourself

The notes box records anything the order itself doesn't say — *"customer asked
for delivery after 6pm"*, *"gift wrap requested on WhatsApp"*.

Notes are internal. Customers never see them. Every note is stamped with who
wrote it and when, so a second person picking up the order knows the history.

### Read the history

The timeline at the bottom lists every status change with who made it and when.
This is your answer whenever someone asks what happened to an order.

## Cancelling an order

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the order and press Cancel order">
    Available at any stage except Delivered and already-Cancelled.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm in the dialog">
    The console asks twice on purpose.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stock returns automatically">
    The pieces go back into your available stock, and the customer is emailed
    that their order was cancelled.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Cancelling can't be undone.** There's no un-cancel. If the customer changes
  their mind, they place a new order.
</Warning>

## Common situations

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The customer wants to change their order">
    There's no editing an order in place. Cancel it and ask them to reorder — or
    for something small like a different plating, note it on the order and sort
    it out when you pack.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The courier lost the parcel">
    Leave the order at Shipped while you chase the courier with the AWB. If it's
    genuinely gone, cancel the order (restoring stock) and settle with the
    customer directly.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A COD parcel came back undelivered">
    Cancel the order. Stock returns automatically. If the same customer keeps
    doing this, [Customers](/insight/customers) flags them with a **Has
    cancelled** mark — worth a confirming phone call before you ship to them
    again.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I marked something Shipped by mistake">
    Step it back one stage. Note that this clears the AWB you entered, so
    you'll re-enter it when you genuinely ship.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="An order has been Pending for days">
    The Dashboard's **Overdue Pending Orders** list exists for exactly this.
    Phone the customer, apologise, then Confirm or cancel. Don't leave them
    hanging.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
