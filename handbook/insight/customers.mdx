Everyone who has ever ordered appears here, one row each. Customers arrive on
this list automatically the moment they place their first order.

## The columns

| Column | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer** | Name. Click the row for their full history. |
| **Phone** | Their number — the fastest way to reach a jewellery customer. |
| **Orders** | How many they've placed. |
| **Lifetime** | Everything they've spent with you, in ₹. |
| **Last order** | How recently they bought. |
| **Reviews** | How many reviews they've written. |
| **Standing** | A flag, when there's something to know. |

Search by name, phone or email at the top, and re-sort the list with the sort
control.

## The standing flags

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Repeat" icon="repeat" color="#1B7A3D">
    Three or more orders. Someone who keeps choosing you.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Has cancelled" icon="triangle-exclamation" color="#B7791F">
    At least one cancelled order.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Most customers carry no flag at all — that just means one or two clean orders,
which is completely normal.

<Warning>
  **Has cancelled outranks Repeat.** On a cash-on-delivery store, an order that
  gets refused at the door costs you shipping both ways. If someone has
  cancelled before, the console tells you *that* first — even if they've also
  spent a lot with you.
</Warning>

## Opening a customer

Click any row for their full picture:

- Their totals — orders, lifetime spend, average order value, cancellation rate
- Every order they've placed, with status, and a link into the order itself
- The reviews they left, shown against the order that prompted them

That last one is the useful bit. You can see not just *that* someone reviewed,
but which purchase made them happy or disappointed.

## Using this well

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="New bridal stock has arrived">
    Sort by lifetime spend. The top of that list has bought from you repeatedly
    and trusts you. A personal WhatsApp with two photos will outperform any
    broadcast.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A large COD order from a new name">
    Check their row before shipping. A high cancellation rate is worth a
    confirming phone call — friendly, thirty seconds, and it saves you a
    round-trip courier charge.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A good customer has gone quiet">
    Sort by last order date. Someone who bought three times and then vanished
    six months ago is worth a message. They may simply have forgotten you
    exist.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Understanding a complaint">
    Open their record before you call back. Knowing they're on their fourth
    order, or that a previous piece was cancelled, changes how the conversation
    should go.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What you can't do here

This screen is **read-only** — a place to understand people, not edit them.
Customers manage their own name, address and password from their account on the
storefront.

<Note>
  Their phone numbers and addresses are private data they trusted you with. Use
  them to serve the order, not to sell to lists.
</Note>
