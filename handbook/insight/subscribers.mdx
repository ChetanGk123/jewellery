People who gave you their email address to hear about new pieces and offers.
Many have never bought anything; they're interested, not yet convinced.

## The list

| Column | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Subscriber** | Their email address. |
| **Source** | Where they signed up from. |
| **Mailing list** | Which list they're on. |
| **Joined** | When they subscribed. |

Search by email at the top.

## Getting the addresses out

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Copy emails" icon="copy">
    Copies every address to your clipboard, ready to paste into your email tool.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Export" icon="file-arrow-down">
    Downloads the list as a file, for a mailing service or your records.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  **Paste addresses into BCC, never To or CC.** Putting two hundred customers in
  the To line shows every one of them every other address. That's a privacy
  breach, and it's the kind people remember.
</Warning>

## Removing someone

**Remove subscriber** takes them off the list, after a confirmation.

<Note>
  If someone asks to be removed, do it that day. Continuing to email a person
  who has asked you to stop is the fastest way to get your emails marked as
  spam — which quietly stops your *order confirmations* reaching anyone.
</Note>

## What to send

The console doesn't send campaigns — it holds the list. Use your own email tool
for the sending.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="New collection arrivals">
    Three or four photos, a line each, a link to the collection. This is the one
    people actually open.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Festival offers">
    Pair it with a [coupon code](/catalogue/coupons) made for the campaign, so
    you can see from its usage count exactly how many sales the email produced.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Restock notices">
    "The piece that sold out is back" is a genuinely useful email, and it sells.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  Once or twice a month, with real photos. A list that hears from you at festival
  time and stays quiet otherwise keeps working for years; one that emails weekly
  gets filtered and forgotten.
</Tip>
