One table, one row per product, telling you what actually sold rather than what
you hoped would.

## The columns

| Column | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Product** | The piece. |
| **Price** | What it currently sells for. |
| **Units** | How many sold in the period you've chosen. |
| **Revenue** | What that came to in ₹. |
| **Stock** | What's left. |
| **Trend** | Whether it's moving up or down. |

Click **History** on any row for its last six months, so you can tell a genuine
decline from a quiet fortnight.

## Choosing a period

Set the **from** and **to** dates at the top. Some periods worth comparing:

## What to do with it

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="High units, low stock">
    Reorder immediately. This is the most expensive row on the page — every day
    it's out of stock is a sale walking away.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="High revenue, low units">
    An expensive piece selling occasionally. Perfectly healthy. Make sure it's
    well photographed and easy to find — it's carrying real money.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Zero units, plenty of stock">
    Money sitting in a drawer. Before writing it off, check the obvious: does it
    have good photos, a real description, the right category? A piece nobody can
    see isn't a piece nobody wants.

    If it's genuinely dead, discount it or set it to Draft.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Falling trend on a former bestseller">
    Open its **History**. A steady six-month decline is real — fashion moved on.
    A sudden drop usually has a cause: it went out of stock, a photo broke, or a
    competitor undercut you.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  Read this once a week, not once a day. Daily numbers on a jewellery store are
  noise; weekly numbers are a pattern.
</Tip>

## Getting here from a product

The **View analytics** action on any row in [Products](/catalogue/products)
brings you straight to that piece's figures — useful when you're mid-way through
deciding whether to reorder.
