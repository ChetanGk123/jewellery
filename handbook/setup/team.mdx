---
title: "Team"
description: "Who can sign in to the admin console."
---

Everyone with access to the console, and the controls to add or remove them.

## Giving someone access

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask them to create an account on your storefront first">
    They sign up as an ordinary customer, with the email address you're about to
    grant. Access is added to an account that already exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type that email into 'Grant admin access'">
    Press **Grant access**. You'll see *"Access granted."*
  </Step>
  <Step title="They sign in at /admin">
    Same email and password they just signed up with.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Admin access is all-or-nothing.** There are no limited roles. Anyone you add
  can change prices, cancel orders, read every customer's phone number and
  address, see your revenue, and remove your access. Add people you'd trust with
  the shop keys.
</Warning>

## Removing access

Press **Remove access** next to their name and confirm. They're out immediately.

Their account still exists as an ordinary customer — you've removed the console
access, not the person.

<Note>
  Do this the same day someone leaves. Not next week.
</Note>

## The record of changes

Below the list is a log of recent access changes — who was granted or removed,
by whom, and when.

This is why individual accounts matter. A shared login tells you *someone*
cancelled an order; individual accounts tell you who, and let you ask them
about it.

## Sensible practice

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="One account per person" icon="user">
    Never a shared login. The record is worthless without it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Keep the list short" icon="users-slash">
    Two or three people. Every extra account is another way in.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Review it occasionally" icon="calendar-check">
    Every few months, read the list. Remove anyone who no longer needs it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Always keep two" icon="key">
    At least two admins, so a forgotten password never locks the business out of
    its own store.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  **Don't remove your own access.** If you're the only admin and you remove
  yourself, nobody can grant it back from inside the console — that needs
  technical help at the database level.
</Warning>
